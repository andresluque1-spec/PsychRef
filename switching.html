The four strategies

StrategyHowUse when
Direct switchStop A today, start B tomorrow at low-moderate doseSame class, similar mechanism, moderate doses (SSRI→SSRI); short total exposure to A; intolerable adverse effect demanding a clean stop
Cross-taperStep A down while stepping B up over 1–4 weeksDifferent mechanisms, fragile patients, high doses, agents with bad discontinuation (paroxetine, venlafaxine). The default when unsure.
Taper then start (washout)Taper A to zero, wait a defined washout, start BMandatory around MAOIs; wise when the combination itself is dangerous (serotonergic stacking, additive QTc)
Overlap & taper ("plateau")Get B fully therapeutic, THEN withdraw A slowlyAntipsychotics in relapse-prone patients; anywhere a coverage gap is the worst outcome
One dial at a time when possible: if you change two drugs and add a supplement in the same month, the improvement — or the rash — has three fathers. Sequence changes so each result is attributable.

MAOI washouts — the hard rules

  • Serotonergic antidepressant → MAOI: washout ≥ 5 half-lives of drug AND active metabolites. Practically: 2 weeks for most SSRIs/SNRIs/TCAs; 5 weeks for fluoxetine (norfluoxetine's half-life).
  • MAOI → serotonergic: wait 2 weeks after stopping the MAOI (enzyme regeneration), including when switching MAOI → MAOI.
  • Bupropion, stimulants, and sympathomimetics also respect the 2-week MAOI boundary (hypertensive risk, not serotonergic).
  • During any MAOI washout period the patient is untreated — plan support, visits, and safety around that window; it's a known relapse trap.
No exceptions exist to the MAOI rules. This is the one switch where "the patient was doing badly so we compressed the washout" appears in malpractice files. Linezolid and methylene blue count as MAOIs when they show up from other specialties.

Antipsychotic switches

  • Default: cross-titrate over 2–4 weeks, or plateau (B therapeutic first) in relapse-prone schizophrenia/schizoaffective patients — a coverage gap costs a hospitalization.
  • Cholinergic rebound: stopping strongly anticholinergic agents (clozapine, olanzapine, quetiapine, chlorpromazine) too fast → nausea, insomnia, restlessness, flu-like misery misread as relapse. Taper these slowly even when the destination drug is already on board.
  • Withdrawal dyskinesia & rebound psychosis: abrupt exits from D2-tight agents can produce transient dyskinesias and, especially with clozapine, rapid rebound psychosis — clozapine discontinuation is its own careful protocol; never abrupt without medical necessity.
  • Switching TO aripiprazole (partial agonist): in patients stable on full antagonists, adding aripiprazole can transiently worsen psychosis or akathisia — plateau approach, patient warned, slower is better.
  • To/from LAIs: overlap oral coverage per each LAI's initiation kinetics; when leaving an LAI remember months of drug remain on board — factor that into "washouts" and adverse-effect attribution.
  • Track the metabolic handoff: a switch olanzapine→aripiprazole is also a metabolic intervention — schedule the weight/A1c follow-up that proves it.

Discontinuation symptoms vs. relapse — the differentiator table

TimingDays (1–7) after dose reduction/stopWeeks later

Teach this table to patients before the switch starts — a forewarned patient rides out day-4 dizziness; a blindsided one declares the new drug a failure and stops everything.
