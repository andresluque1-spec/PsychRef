Behavioral Activation (Brief BA)

The highest evidence-per-minute intervention for depression in a med-management visit. Validated in protocols as brief as two sessions; fits inside a 90833 add-on.

Why BA, and the 60-second rationale

Model: depression shrinks the world — withdrawal removes contact with reward, which deepens depression. BA reverses the loop: action first, motivation follows. Outcomes for BA alone rival full CBT and antidepressants for mild–moderate depression; brief 2–4 contact protocols are validated in primary-care settings.

Best candidates: depression with withdrawal/anergia, adjustment disorders, depression + chronic pain, patients who find "thought work" abstract. Works while waiting for medication onset — give patients something to do in weeks 0–4.

"When people get depressed, they naturally pull back — less activity, fewer people, staying in. The problem is that pulling back feeds the depression. We're not going to wait for motivation to come back. We're going to schedule small activities first, because in depression, action comes before motivation, not after. The medication works from the inside; this works from the outside."

Session 1 — inside a 30-minute combined visit

MinMove
0–12E/M portion: interval history, scales, med review, plan. Close the med loop first so the therapy block is uninterrupted.
12–15Bridge + rationale script (above). Ask: "What's one thing you used to do regularly that's dropped off since you've been feeling this way?"
15–22Values → activities. Pick ONE life area (connection, body, mastery, pleasure). Generate 2–3 candidate activities. Shrink until success is near-certain: not "go to the gym" — "walk to the mailbox after lunch."
22–27Schedule it. Specific: what, when, where, with whom. Anchor to an existing habit ("after my morning coffee"). Rate confidence 0–10; if <7, shrink the task, not the goal. Set a backup plan for the most likely obstacle.
27–30Summarize, have THEM say the plan back, write it on the after-visit summary. Confirm you'll review it first thing next visit.
The one rule: the activity must be scheduled (day + time), not intended. "I'll try to walk more" predicts nothing. "Tuesday and Thursday after dropping the kids, 10 minutes around the block" is a prescription.

Follow-up visits — the 5-minute BA review

  1. Review first, always. "Last time you planned X — how did it go?" Skipping the review teaches patients homework doesn't matter.
  2. If done: reinforce effort, ask what they noticed in mood before vs. after the activity (builds the action→mood link). Then expand slightly — frequency before intensity.
  3. If not done: zero criticism. "That's useful information — what got in the way?" Treat it as a task-design problem: too big, wrong time, no anchor. Shrink and re-schedule.
  4. Graph it against their PHQ-9 when scores improve: "Notice your score dropped the same month you got back to the garage projects. That's not a coincidence."

Troubleshooting

  • "I don't enjoy anything anymore." Anhedonia is expected — enjoyment is not the assignment, contact is. "Do it as an experiment, and just notice. We're collecting data, not chasing fun yet."
  • "I don't have the energy." Shrink until the energy objection disappears. There is always a version small enough.
  • "I'll do it when I feel better." Return to the rationale: in depression the sequence is reversed — action produces the feeling.
  • Did everything, felt nothing: check dosing (once is not a trial), check whether the activity ties to a value, screen for untreated insomnia or substance use flattening reward.
  • Severe/melancholic depression or active SI: BA is an adjunct, not a substitute — anchor to med optimization, safety planning, and level-of-care decisions.

Español — frases clave (forma usted)

La lógica: "Cuando uno está deprimido, es natural retirarse — menos actividad, menos gente. Pero eso alimenta la depresión. No vamos a esperar a que regrese la motivación. Vamos a programar actividades pequeñas primero, porque en la depresión, la acción viene antes que la motivación."
Elegir la actividad: "¿Qué es algo que usted hacía con regularidad y que ha dejado de hacer desde que se siente así?" · "Vamos a hacerlo tan pequeño que sea casi imposible fallar."
Programar: "¿Qué día? ¿A qué hora? ¿Con quién?" · "Del cero al diez, ¿qué tan seguro está de que lo va a hacer?" (si <7: "Vamos a hacerlo más pequeño.")
Revisión: "La última vez planeamos ___. ¿Cómo le fue?" · "¿Qué se le atravesó?" · "¿Notó algún cambio en su ánimo antes y después de la actividad?"

Chart-ready documentation

Session 1: Provided brief behavioral activation therapy (16+ minutes psychotherapy in addition to E/M). Reviewed behavioral model of depression linking withdrawal to symptom maintenance; patient identified reduced engagement in [valued area]. Collaboratively selected and scheduled specific activity: [activity, day/time]. Anticipated barrier of [barrier] addressed with [backup plan]. Patient verbalized understanding of rationale and confidence of [X]/10 in completing the plan. Will review adherence and mood response at next visit.
Follow-up: Continued behavioral activation focus during psychotherapy portion. Reviewed prior activity assignment: patient [completed/partially completed/did not complete] scheduled activity. [If completed: patient noted (mood observation); plan expanded to (next step).] [If not: identified barrier of (barrier); assignment restructured to (smaller/re-anchored task).] Reinforced action-before-motivation model. PHQ-9 trend reviewed with patient in relation to activity level.
QA check: note names the modality (behavioral activation), the specific technique used, minutes of psychotherapy distinct from E/M, and links it to a treatment-plan goal — the four elements your 90833 needs.
