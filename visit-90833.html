The 90833 Visit Architecture

How to structure, deliver, and document real psychotherapy inside a combined E/M + therapy visit — compliantly and repeatably.

The rules in five lines

  • 90833 = 16–37 minutes of psychotherapy performed with an E/M service (99212–99215, 99205/99214 in your pattern). 90836 = 38–52 minutes. Never billed alone.
  • Therapy time must be distinct from E/M time — the minutes counting toward psychotherapy cannot be the same minutes used to select the E/M level.
  • The E/M level is chosen by medical decision making, not time, when a psychotherapy add-on is billed.
  • Document a good-faith time estimate for the therapy portion, the named modality, the technique(s) used, and the link to a treatment-plan goal.
  • A total visit of ~30 minutes is the realistic floor for a defensible combined visit.
Audit red flags: 90833 on nearly 100% of visits; identical therapy paragraphs across patients or across visits (copy-paste carryover); therapy time that is arithmetically impossible given the schedule; "supportive therapy provided" with no technique, content, or response described.

Why the pivot sentence matters: it marks the boundary between E/M and therapy time in real life, which makes your time attestation honest and your AI scribe's job easy — tell Open Evidence to treat everything after the pivot as the psychotherapy portion.

What counts as psychotherapy (and what doesn't)

CountsDoes NOT count
Named-modality interventions: behavioral activation, cognitive restructuring, MI (rolling with resistance, change talk), SFBT scaling/miracle question, exposure planning, CBT-I stimulus control coaching, supportive techniques applied deliberately (reframing, strengthening coping), safety-plan construction as a therapeutic intervention Med education and consent discussion; reviewing side effects; scale administration; care coordination; general rapport and empathic listening alone; lifestyle advice without a therapeutic frame; scheduling

The honest test: could you name the technique, what you targeted, and how the patient responded? If yes, it was psychotherapy. If the note could only say "we talked," it was E/M counseling.

Split-treatment etiquette (patient has an outside therapist)

  • Don't run a competing therapy. If they're doing trauma work with an LMHC, your therapy minutes support that work and the pharmacotherapy — adherence, skills reinforcement, psychoeducation — rather than opening a parallel modality.
  • Appropriate targets in split treatment: motivation for and healthy use of medication, reinforcing the therapist's homework, crisis/safety skills, sleep behavioral work if the therapist isn't doing it.
  • Name the division of labor to the patient: "Your therapist is leading the trauma work; my job is the medication and making sure everything supports what you two are building."
  • Document coordination: "Patient continues weekly therapy with [name/discipline]; today's psychotherapy portion focused on [non-overlapping target] in support of ongoing treatment."

Drop-in note paragraph (your format)

Psychotherapy (add-on, 90833): In addition to E/M services, provided [16–20] minutes of individual psychotherapy using [modality]. Session focused on [specific target tied to treatment plan goal]. Technique: [technique — e.g., reviewed activity scheduling adherence and restructured assignment / elicited change talk regarding alcohol reduction / conducted scaling exercise on return-to-work goal]. Patient response: [engaged/receptive; specific observation or quote]. Plan: [between-visit assignment], to be reviewed at next visit. Psychotherapy time was separate and distinct from time spent on evaluation and management.
Scribe prompt line to add: "Generate a distinct 'Psychotherapy' section that names the modality, the specific technique used after the provider says a pivot phrase like 'let's shift to the therapy piece,' the patient's response, and the between-visit assignment. Never reuse psychotherapy language from prior notes."

Pre-signature self-audit — six boxes

  • Time attested for the therapy portion (16–37 min) and stated as distinct from E/M
  • Modality named
  • Specific technique + content this visit (would fail a copy-paste comparison with last note)
  • Tied to a treatment-plan goal
  • Patient response documented
  • E/M level justified by MDM on its own — the visit stands even if the add-on were removed
