Serotonin Syndrome vs. NMS

The two hyperthermic drug emergencies you must never confuse — because the treatments diverge and the exam tells them apart in under a minute.

Hunter criteria (serotonin syndrome) — memorize the branch

Serotonergic agent on board plus any ONE of:

  • Spontaneous clonus
  • Inducible clonus + agitation or diaphoresis
  • Ocular clonus + agitation or diaphoresis
  • Tremor + hyperreflexia
  • Hypertonia + temp >38°C + ocular or inducible clonus

Exam move you can do (and teach patients' families to describe by phone): dorsiflex the ankle briskly and hold — rhythmic beating = inducible clonus. On video visits, watch the eyes for slow horizontal oscillations (ocular clonus) and ask about new leg jerks, sweating, and diarrhea after any med change.

Mimics to hold in mind: anticholinergic toxicity (dry, flushed, urinary retention, NO clonus — "dry" vs. serotonin's "wet"), malignant hyperthermia (anesthesia context), catatonia (overlaps NMS; consider a lorazepam challenge), sedative-hypnotic withdrawal, stimulant intoxication, CNS infection. Malignant catatonia and NMS can be near-indistinguishable — treat supportively and involve psychiatry + medicine together.

Outpatient/telehealth playbook

  1. Recognize the setup, not just the syndrome: your risk moments are cross-titrations, adding tramadol from an urgent care, linezolid from a hospitalization, dextromethorphan self-medication, and the patient restarting an old MAOI-era med. The SS phone call usually starts "ever since we raised the dose Tuesday…"
  2. Triage by severity: mild (tremor + hyperreflexia, afebrile, walking and talking normally) → hold serotonergics, arrange same-day or next-morning evaluation, explicit return precautions. Anything with fever, clonus, confusion, or autonomic instability → ED now, and call ahead: "suspected serotonin syndrome, currently on [agents]" — name the syndrome to the triage nurse.
  3. For suspected NMS there is no mild pathway: rigidity + fever + mental status change on an antipsychotic = ED, period, with the medication list transmitted.
  4. After the event: the rechallenge decision (which agent, how long to wait, what to avoid combining) is a documented, supervised decision — loop in your supervising psychiatrist. Post-NMS antipsychotic rechallenge: wait ≥2 weeks, different (lower-potency or atypical) agent, slow titration, informed consent documented.
Prevention beats diagnosis: before any serotonergic addition, run the full list — including OTC (dextromethorphan), pain clinic scripts (tramadol), and supplements (St. John's wort). Your EFORCSE check catches controlled substances; it will not catch cough syrup.

Documentation

Patient reported [symptoms] beginning [timeframe] after [med change]. Assessment via [phone/video]: [findings — e.g., fine tremor, no reported jerking movements, afebrile by home thermometer, ambulating normally, no confusion per patient and spouse]. Suspected [mild serotonergic toxicity vs. alternative]; instructed to hold [agents], provided explicit return precautions (fever, muscle jerking, confusion, worsening sweating/diarrhea → ED immediately), arranged [same-day/next-day] evaluation. [If ED referred: ED notified by phone of suspected serotonin syndrome/NMS and current medication list.] Supervising psychiatrist notified. Rechallenge plan to be determined after resolution.
