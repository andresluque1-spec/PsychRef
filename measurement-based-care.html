The four words that drive decisions

TermOperational definitionClinical meaning
Reliable changePHQ-9 or GAD-7 change of ≥5 pointsMovement beyond measurement noise — the minimum clinically important difference. A 3-point drop is not evidence the plan is working.
Response≥50% reduction from the pre-treatment baselineThe plan is working; continue and consolidate.
RemissionPHQ-9 < 5 · GAD-7 < 5The target. Response without remission still carries relapse risk — residual symptoms are the strongest predictor of recurrence.
Non-response<20–25% improvement after an adequate trial (adequate dose × 4–6 weeks)Change something: dose, agent, augmentation, therapy intensity, or the diagnosis itself.
Anchor everything to the pre-treatment baseline, not last visit's score. A patient who went 22 → 16 → 15 → 15 looks "stable" visit-to-visit but is a 32% improver overall — a partial response stalled short of target, which is an action signal, not a maintenance signal.

Treat-to-target algorithm (each follow-up)

  1. Score obtained and graphed? If no — get it now; trend beats snapshot. Two data points is a line; one is a dot.
  2. Item 9 / safety items first. Any endorsement → C-SSRS and, if indicated, the Safety Plan page. This precedes everything else.
  3. Compare to baseline:
    • Remission → consolidate, discuss maintenance duration (first episode: 6–12 months at full dose after remission; multiple episodes: longer), relapse early-warning signs.
    • Response, not remission → optimize: dose to top of tolerated range, add the behavioral module targeting the residual symptom (insomnia → CBT-I; inactivity → BA), before switching agents.
    • Reliable change but <50% at 4–6 weeks adequate dose → increase dose or augment; recheck in 4 weeks.
    • Non-response after adequate trial → verify adherence and diagnosis (bipolarity screen, substance use, thyroid/anemia, OSA), then switch or augment. Two adequate failed trials → treatment-resistance workup and psychotherapy referral if not already in place.
  4. Discuss the score with the patient — the most-skipped step, and the one that makes MBC work (scripts below).
  5. Chart the decision the score produced. A score documented without a linked decision is data collection, not measurement-based care.
Discordance rule: when the score and the patient's story disagree, the interview wins — but investigate the gap. Improving score + "I feel terrible" → look for a problem the PHQ-9 doesn't measure (anxiety, pain, marriage, meaning). Terrible score + "I'm fine" → minimization, misread items, or someone else filled it out in the waiting room.

Score conversations

Improving

"Your score went from 18 to 9 since we started — that's a real response, more than half. And look at when it moved: the drop starts the month you got back to walking. You did that part, not the pill."

Flat (the hard one)

"I want to show you something. Six weeks on a full dose and your score hasn't really moved — 16, 15, 16. To me that says this plan isn't strong enough yet, and I don't want to waste another month of your life on it. Here are our options..."

Worsening

"Your number went the wrong direction this month, from 11 to 17. Before we blame the medication — what happened in the last four weeks? Sometimes the score is measuring life, not the treatment."

Response-but-not-remission plateau

"You're genuinely better — but 'better' and 'well' aren't the same thing, and the leftover symptoms are what predict relapse. I don't want to park at halfway. Here's what closing that gap could look like."

Eso me dice que este plan todavía no es suficiente, y no quiero gastar otro mes de su vida en algo que no está funcionando." · "'Mejor' y 'bien' no son lo mismo — los síntomas que quedan son los que predicen una recaída."

Pitfalls

  • Treating the number instead of the patient — scores inform decisions; they don't make them. Function, side effects, and patient priorities share the table.
  • Score inflation/deflation with the audience — patients may soften scores to please you or inflate them fearing med discontinuation. Naming this openly ("answer for the worst days, not for me") improves data quality.
  • Somatic-item contamination — sleep, energy, appetite items elevate PHQ-9 in medical illness, pregnancy, and chemotherapy; weight the cognitive/affective items in those patients.
  • Language/literacy — the validated Spanish PHQ-9/GAD-7 exist; use them rather than translating aloud on the fly, which changes the instrument.
  • Frequency — every visit for active treatment; monthly minimum during acute-phase changes. Annual scores tell you nothing you can act on.
  • The abandoned graph — if scores are collected but never shown to the patient, half of MBC's effect (patient activation) is left unused.

Chart-ready documentation

PHQ-9 today: [X] ([severity band]), from pre-treatment baseline of [Y] — a [Z]% change, consistent with [remission / response without remission / partial improvement / non-response] after [N] weeks at [dose]. Item 9: [negative / positive → C-SSRS completed, see risk assessment]. Score trend reviewed graphically with the patient, who [concurred / noted discordance, explored as follows: ...]. Based on measured trajectory, plan: [continue and consolidate / dose optimization to X / augmentation with Y / switch to Z / added behavioral target: (module)]. Will remeasure at next visit in [interval].
