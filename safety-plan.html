Safety Planning Intervention

Stanley-Brown SPI — the evidence-based step before (and after) the Baker Act. A brief intervention, not a form: build it collaboratively, in the patient's own words.

When to use it

  • Any positive C-SSRS ideation, recent attempt or self-harm, or discharge from a receiving facility — SPI plus structured follow-up reduced suicidal behavior vs. usual care in the 2018 JAMA Psychiatry ED trial.
  • Elevated but sub-Baker-Act risk: criteria not met, but you're not comfortable ending the visit with nothing in place.
  • Post-Baker-Act re-entry visit — rebuild the plan together; the crisis version made at the facility rarely survives contact with home life.
  • Not a no-suicide contract. Contracts have no evidence and false reassurance value. SPI replaces them.
Time & billing: a well-done SPI takes 15–25 minutes — it IS the psychotherapy portion of that visit. Name it in the note as a crisis-focused therapeutic intervention.

The six steps, with elicitation scripts

1
Warning signs — personal signals a crisis is building (thoughts, images, moods, behaviors).
"How will you know when the plan should be used? What do you notice in yourself right before things get that dark?"
2
Internal coping strategies — things they can do alone to distract: walk, shower, music, game, gym, prayer.
"What can you do, on your own, to take your mind off things — even for 20 minutes?"
3
People and places for distraction — social contact without disclosing the crisis: the gym, a coffee shop, a cousin who talks football.
"Who helps you feel less alone just by being around? Where can you go where there are people?"
4
People to ask for help — those they'd actually tell. Confirm the person knows and agrees, when feasible.
"If the feelings kept building, who could you tell what's really going on?"
5
Professionals and agencies — your practice's number and after-hours line, 988 (call/text), Suicide & Crisis Lifeline chat, nearest ER.
6
Making the environment safe — lethal means counseling (below). Even without a stated plan, means safety buys time for the crisis to pass.
Quality markers: the patient's own words, specific names and numbers, and a final ask — "Where will you keep this? How likely are you to use it, 0–10?" If <7, ask what would make it more usable and revise now.

Lethal means counseling — the Florida conversation

Firearms (lead with respect, not restriction)

"I ask everyone this because it's the single strongest thing families can do: are there firearms at home? ... I'm not asking you to give anything up permanently. During this rough stretch, the goal is time and distance — a crisis peaks and passes, often in under an hour. Options people choose: storing them with a trusted person, a gun shop or range that offers storage, or locked with ammunition separate and someone else holding the key."
  • Offer the menu, let them pick — collaboration predicts follow-through. Involve a family member in the plan when possible, and set a specific plan: who moves them, by when.
  • Document what was agreed, not just "counseled on means."

Medications

  • Limit quantities dispensed during high-risk periods (14-day fills), have a family member hold and dispense, lock boxes for the rest.
  • Flag your own prescribing: avoid stockpiling TCAs, lithium without supervision, or large benzo supplies in at-risk patients.

Other

  • Ask directly about the method they've thought about — restrict that one specifically and first.
  • Alcohol is an accelerant: reducing access/planning around drinking belongs in step 6.
If the patient refuses all means-safety steps and risk is elevated: that refusal is itself risk data. Re-weigh level of care, involve family per consent, document your reasoning — and if criteria are met, the Baker Act page is one tab away.

Fillable plan — complete with the patient, then print

1. My warning signs / Mis señales de alerta

2. Things I can do on my own / Lo que puedo hacer por mi cuenta

3. People & places that take my mind off things / Personas y lugares que me distraen

4. People I can ask for help / Personas a las que puedo pedir ayuda

5. Professionals I can call / Profesionales a los que puedo llamar

6. Making my environment safe / Hacer mi entorno más seguro

One thing worth living for / Algo por lo que vale la pena vivir

Fields are not saved — print or photograph the completed plan before closing the page. Have the patient photograph it on their phone as well.

Español — frases de elicitación (forma usted)

Señales: "¿Cómo sabrá usted que es momento de usar el plan? ¿Qué nota en sí mismo justo antes de que las cosas se pongan tan oscuras?"
Afrontamiento: "¿Qué puede hacer usted, por su cuenta, para distraerse — aunque sea por veinte minutos?"
Apoyo: "Si los sentimientos siguieran creciendo, ¿a quién le podría contar lo que realmente está pasando?"
Medios letales: "Le pregunto esto a todos mis pacientes porque es lo más importante que una familia puede hacer: ¿hay armas de fuego en casa? ... No le pido que renuncie a nada de forma permanente. Durante esta etapa difícil, la meta es tiempo y distancia — una crisis llega a su punto máximo y pasa."
Cierre: "¿Dónde va a guardar este plan?" · "Del cero al diez, ¿qué tan probable es que lo use?"

Chart-ready documentation

Completed collaborative Safety Planning Intervention (Stanley-Brown model) as the psychotherapy focus of today's visit ([X] minutes, distinct from E/M). Patient identified personal warning signs of [signs], internal coping strategies including [strategies], social supports [names/relationships], and professional contacts including this office and 988. Lethal means counseling completed: [firearms disposition — e.g., patient agreed to transfer firearms to brother's residence by tomorrow evening; wife present and confirmed]; medication supply limited to 14-day fill. Patient rated likelihood of using the plan [X]/10, retained a printed and photographed copy. Follow-up scheduled [interval]; patient verbalized agreement to contact 988 or present to the ER if warning signs escalate before then.
Reference only. Model: Stanley & Brown, Cogn Behav Pract 2012; SPI+follow-up: JAMA Psychiatry 2018. Free clinician training: suicidesafetyplan.com and the CALM (Counseling on Access to Lethal Means) course.
