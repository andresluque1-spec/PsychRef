Track verified changes to the psychiatry reference and check current prescribing information at the point of care.

Review status: in progress

The medication catalog is a static reference. Its entries have not all been checked against current labels. A date on this page describes specific corrections below, not a full review of every medication. Verify any patient-specific choice in the current FDA label, interaction resources, and your local protocols.

Verified corrections · September 26, 2026

  • Clozapine: FDA removed its REMS program effective June 13, 2025. The site no longer presents REMS enrollment or registry submission as a prerequisite. Monitoring for severe neutropenia remains clinically important. FDA communication.
  • Pregnancy letter categories: FDA replaced A/B/C/D/X categories with narrative labeling. Medication cards and generated handouts now direct readers to current pregnancy and lactation labeling rather than showing legacy categories. FDA explanation.

These corrections do not verify other claims in the catalog, comparison tables, or monitoring pages. A fuller entry-by-entry review is pending.

Where to check changes

  • Drugs@FDA — approvals, current product labels, supplements, and approval history.
  • FDA novel drug approvals — screen for new agents; check the actual label before adding an entry.
  • FDA Drug Safety Communications — new warnings and safety actions.
  • FDA safety labeling changes — changed warnings, contraindications, and precautions.
  • DailyMed — current submitted labeling; confirm formulation and manufacturer.

Review procedure

  1. Check recent approvals and safety communications monthly; check a drug's current label whenever making a clinical decision.
  2. For each candidate change, confirm generic and brand names, formulation, indication and population, dose range, contraindications, boxed warnings, interactions, and monitoring directly in the label.
  3. Update the medication entry, related algorithms, comparison tables, monitoring pages, and generated handout together. Record the source URL and review date.
  4. Have a clinician review the change before relying on it in care. Mark unreviewed content clearly; do not equate “no new approval” with “no label change.”

This page does not run an automatic FDA feed. An automated discovery check can flag candidates, but label interpretation and site edits require verification.
